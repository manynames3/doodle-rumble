# Developer and market review — v0.4.0

Assessment of the current build, 2026-09-13. These are provisional editorial scores, not measured customer satisfaction or an independent certification. The reviewer helped implement the game; that is a real source of bias. To limit it, this review separates verified behavior, visible shortcomings and untested assumptions.

| Perspective | Current score | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Senior developer: family prototype | **7/10** | A coherent family playtest build with meaningful defensive and arena choices; refinement remains. |
| Market researcher: readiness to sell | **4/10** | A product hypothesis with a playable demonstration, not demonstrated demand or competitive polish. |

## Developer assessment

The underlying game works: six playable fighters, three illustrated environments, a six-opponent story, local two-player play, remapping, health/cooldowns, rounds, rematches, stage music and comfort controls. The new bosses have distinct patterns, commitment warnings and recovery windows. Fixed-step damage and explicit recovery protection are sound choices. Automated and graphical runtime checks cover much more than simply launching a scene.

The latest pass addresses three real weaknesses: each fighter now has a timed dodge/counter and a distinct aerial basic; the arenas have different reachable layouts plus a springboard, crumbling bridge or jump portals; and acting poses/ink treatment are more consistent. These create choices about timing, height and position. The developer score rises from 6 to 7 for implemented, verified design improvements—not for assumed player enjoyment. Combat still offers a relatively small move set, and the new interactions have not been tested for long-term balance or dominant strategies. Story Mode remains an ordered arcade ladder without developed narrative scenes or persistent journey progress.

The title poster and illustrated cards remain more painterly and detailed than the moving characters and interactive platforms. The H4CK3R camera rig, ragged Dark lord, individual fighter stances, fuller marker strokes, aligned swing phases and surface-projected shadows are closer to the supplied direction. A screenshot still is not evidence of convincing animation across an entire session. Character proportions, hitbox alignment, effect density and motion need continued human review. The project also concentrates a lot of presentation and coordination in large scripts, which will become costly to maintain as the roster grows.

Stronger bosses now punish passive play. That demonstrates pressure, not ideal difficulty. Input bots do not establish whether a child understands a warning, feels a loss was fair or asks for a rematch. Physical-controller coverage and long-session testing are still missing. Music has distinct arrangements; listener preference and fatigue have not been measured.

## Market assessment

The competition sets a demanding baseline. [Stick Fight: The Game](https://store.steampowered.com/app/674940/Stick_Fight_The_Game/?l=english) offers 2–4-player local/online physics combat, interactive levels, community levels and Mac support. [Brawlhalla](https://store.steampowered.com/app/291550/Brawlhalla/?l=english) offers free-to-play local/online fighting, a large roster and cross-platform play, including Mac. [Boomerang Fu](https://store.steampowered.com/app/965680/Boomerang_Fu/?l=english) demonstrates how a small input set can support power-up combinations, varied arenas and broad party-game replay value. These are official product claims, not proof of Doodle Rumble's potential sales.

“Fighting characters inspired by children's drawings” gives Doodle Rumble an understandable family identity. Its strongest potential distinction is child authorship and imagination. At present, that distinction is mostly the game's origin and look; children cannot substantially author their own fighting characters or influence the story. Mac availability alone is not a sufficient advantage against established Mac-compatible alternatives.

Commercial-readiness remains 4/10 despite the implementation progress: there is no measured retention, willingness to pay, acquisition cost, demand or audience size for this build. No revenue forecast or confident price recommendation is justified. Public-release asset/character rights are also unverified, especially for recognizable existing characters such as Pac-Man. This review makes no legal determination.

## Best next investment

1. Observe at least five families playing without coaching. Record time to first match, confusing controls, avoidable versus unexplained deaths, voluntary rematches and where children stop.
2. Test the new dodge/counter, aerial moves and three arena interactions for understandable risk/reward, dominant strategies and fair losses. Tune this small set before adding more moves or characters.
3. Close the visual gap between the poster and actual play, then verify controller use, audio comfort and a complete long session on target Macs.

The build is ready for focused family playtesting. Calling it a finished commercial arcade game would overstate both its polish and the available evidence.
